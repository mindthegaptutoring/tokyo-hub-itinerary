Kasai-Rinkai Park (Tuesday, October 20)

Meeting Time: 11:00 AM
Meeting Point: Kasai-Rinkai Park Playground — open in Google Maps
Nearest Station: Kasai-Rinkai-koen Station (JR Keiyo Line)

Map tiles © Esri — pins are approximate; use the Google Maps links below for exact routing.

🍱 Lunch: Picnic lunch or buy in the park — bring your own, or grab something from the on-site Starbucks, PARKLIFE CAFE & RESTAURANT, or the BBQ Square (reservation required for BBQ grills) near the station.

This is an open, choose-your-own-adventure day — the whole park is free to enter and explore, and everything below is optional. Below is the full menu of what's here so the group can decide in the moment.

Free to Explore (No Ticket Needed)

Kasai-Rinkai Park (grounds, lawns & playground)

Our easy, arrival-day hangout — a huge waterfront park with wide lawns, a big playground, and views of Tokyo Bay. Plenty of room for kids to run while parents settle in and start connecting.

Free | Grounds open 24 hours

Bird Sanctuary

A quiet nature reserve covering about a third of the park, with two ponds, observation buildings, and viewing windows onto migratory and resident water birds. A calm counterpoint to the busier parts of the park.

Free | Observation buildings roughly follow park facility hours (~9:00 AM–5:00 PM)

📍 View on Google Maps

Kasai Marine Park Beach (Nishi Nagisa)

A coastal walking area and artificial sandy tidal zone right along Tokyo Bay — good for a barefoot wander or just watching the water.

Free | Open access, no set hours

📍 View on Google Maps

Crystal View Rest House

A striking glass-walled observation building on the park's highest point, with panoramic 2nd-floor views toward Tokyo Bay and, on clear days, the Boso Peninsula. Good shaded resting spot with a small café.

Free | 9:00 AM–5:00 PM

📍 View on Google Maps

Canoe Slalom Centre (viewing only)

A former Tokyo 2020 Olympic venue right next to the park — a 200m artificial white-water course. Fun to watch from the 2nd-floor terrace even without booking anything; canoe/rafting sessions can be booked separately online if anyone's interested.

Free to view from public areas | 9:00 AM–5:00 PM daily

⚠️ If anyone wants to actually book a canoe/rafting session (not just watch), that needs a separate online reservation — not included in this itinerary.

📍 View on Google Maps

Paid Activities

Tokyo Sea Life Park (Aquarium)

Known for its glass-dome entrance and a large tuna tank where the fish swim in a continuous ring overhead — a favorite with younger kids.

General: ¥700 | Ages 65+: ¥350 | Junior high (outside Tokyo): ¥250 | Elementary & younger, and Tokyo junior high students: Free

Hours: 9:30 AM–5:00 PM (last entry 4:00 PM)

Diamond and Flower Ferris Wheel

One of the largest Ferris wheels in Japan (117m), right next to the aquarium — a slow 17-minute rotation with great bay and skyline views. Great low-key activity for mixed ages.

Ages 3+ (standard rate): ¥800 | Ages 2 & under: Free | Seniors 70+: ¥400 | Disabled + one attendant: ¥350 | 10% off with a same-day Sea Life Park ticket

Hours: 10:00 AM–7:00 PM on weekdays (our visit is a Tuesday), 10:00 AM–8:00 PM weekends/holidays

Park Train

A fun, low-effort way to see more of the park — a locomotive-shaped ride that loops slowly around the grounds for about 25 minutes. Nice option for anyone who's tired of walking.

Approx. ¥300 per ride

🍱 Lunch: Free choice after the Science Museum — grab something nearby, or bring a picnic to eat on the lawns of Kitanomaru Park or the East Gardens before the 1:00 PM tour meeting.

Activity Details

Science Museum (10:00 AM)

A family-friendly science center in Kitanomaru Park, just north of the Palace grounds — four floors of hands-on, touchable exhibits, plus daily experiment shows and craft workshops. Stroller-friendly with elevators between floors.

Adults ¥700 | Junior/senior high ¥400 | Elementary school & under ¥250

Hours: 9:00 AM–4:50 PM (last entry 4:00 PM)

📍 View Science Museum on Google Maps

Imperial Palace Official Guided Tour (1:30 PM)

A free, ~75-minute walking tour run by the Imperial Household Agency through parts of the inner palace grounds that are otherwise closed to the public — including the Fujimi-yagura watchtower, the Kyuden Totei Plaza (where the Emperor delivers the New Year's address), the Chowaden Reception Hall, and Nijubashi Bridge. It's a viewing tour from the outside (no entry into buildings), with English audio-guide support.

Free | Reservation required (see box above) | ID: original passport for every attendee

⚠️ Arrive with real margin — security screening and check-in at Kikyo-mon Gate takes time, and the tour starts on the dot. Build in extra buffer for a group our size. Every attendee must bring their actual passport (no photocopies) to check in.

Shinjuku Chuo Park

A large, green urban park in the shadow of Shinjuku's skyscrapers, with strong playgrounds — a good stretch of unstructured play time after the tour wraps up, and a good hangout spot before and after dinner too.

Free

Nearest Station: Tochomae Station (Toei Oedo Line)
🍽️ Dinner: Odakyu Halc (Shinjuku West Exit) — a few minutes' walk from the park, with several restaurant floors to choose from. Not a shared-seating food court, but the closest easy option to regroup for dinner without trekking to Shinjuku Station's south side.

📍 View Shinjuku Chuo Park on Google Maps

TOKYO Night & Light — Projection Mapping Show (~7:30 PM)

A free digital art show projected onto the east face of the Tocho building — Guinness World Records-certified as the largest permanent architectural projection mapping in the world. A relaxed way to close out the day right where the group is already standing.

Free | No reservation needed

October hours: 6:00 PM–9:30 PM, light shows roughly every 15 minutes (about 8 showings a night)

📍 View Tocho East Plaza on Google Maps

Activity Details

Tokyo DisneySea

A full day at Disney's most distinctive park — nautical and exploration-themed "ports" rather than classic Disneyland lands, including the newer Fantasy Springs area. A self-directed day: families move at their own pace and regroup as they'd like.

1-Day Passport (confirmed for Oct 22, 2026): Adult (18+) ¥10,900 | Junior (12–17) ¥9,000 | Child (4–11) ¥5,600 | Ages 3 & under: Free

🎫 Buy tickets here: plan.tokyodisneyresort.jp. Unlike the Palace tour, this isn't a single group booking, date-based tickets sell out on popular days and each family buys their own passports, so families should be told to book directly and early rather than waiting on a group link.

Meiji Shrine & Yoyogi Park (10:00 AM)

A calm, forested start to the day — Meiji Shrine's main grounds are free to wander, with a real change of pace from the city outside. Yoyogi Park sits right next door for open lawns and running-around room before the group heads into Shibuya's energy for the afternoon.

Free (Inner Garden and Treasure Museum charge separately, not part of this stop) | Yoyogi Park: Free

October hours: 5:40 AM–4:40 PM

📍 View Meiji Shrine on Google Maps

Shibuya Crossing

The world's busiest pedestrian intersection — genuinely fun to just stand and watch, and a classic "we're really in Tokyo" moment for kids.

Free

Mega Don Quijote

A multi-story discount department store packed with snacks, cosmetics, toys, and novelty items — chaotic in the best way, and a favorite browsing stop for teens especially.

Free to browse; purchases at your own discretion

Karaoke Pasela (Shibuya) — 3:00–5:00 PM

¥1,580 per adult | Half price (¥790) for kids 12 and under | 2-hour room block

Asakusa Cultural Day (Saturday, October 24)

Daytime Meeting Time: 11:00 AM
Daytime Meeting Point: Asakusa Station — open in Google Maps
Nearest Station: Asakusa Station (Tokyo Metro Ginza Line; Toei Asakusa Line; Tobu Skytree Line)
Evening Meeting Time: ~7:30 PM (flexible — whenever the group finishes dinner; sunset is around 5:00 PM in late October, so it'll already be dark)
Evening Meeting Point: Kaminarimon Gate, Senso-ji Temple — open in Google Maps
Nearest Station: Asakusa Station, same as the daytime meeting point

Map tiles © Esri — pins are approximate; use the Google Maps links below for exact routing.

🍱 Lunch: Not yet arranged — Nakamise shopping street has plenty of street food between the two activities, which could work well as a casual group lunch.

This is a wander-between-stops kind of day — after we all gather at Asakusa Station, move at your own pace. Aim to be at the Tokyo Samurai Ninja Museum by about 12:00–12:30 PM, or grab lunch first and join the 2:00 PM crowd instead. Ganso Sample Making is optional, but everyone should still pop into the Ganso store to see the food-sample displays, even if you're not booked into a workshop session — the items on display are worth a look either way. We'll have dinner in the area afterward, then head to the temple for the evening visit.

Activity Details

Taikokan (Drum Museum)

A small, hands-on museum on the 4th floor of Miyamoto Unosuke Shoten, a taiko drum maker founded in 1861. About 200 drums are on permanent display — Japanese taiko alongside African, European, and American percussion — and most of them (any marked with a musical note symbol) are fair game to actually play. Great opener for the day since it's genuinely interactive rather than look-but-don't-touch. It's a short walk from Asakusa Station, in the same Nishi-Asakusa neighborhood as our Ganso stop later.

Adults ~¥500 | Children ~¥150

Hours: 11:00 AM–4:00 PM (last admission 3:00 PM) | Closed Mondays & Tuesdays — not a concern for our Saturday visit

Address: 4F, 2-1-1 Nishi-Asakusa, Taito-ku  |  Nearest Station: Tawaramachi Station (Ginza Line), ~2 min walk

📍 View Taikokan on Google Maps

Tokyo Samurai Ninja Museum

A hands-on, guided museum experience where kids (and parents) can try on samurai armor, throw shuriken, and learn some real history behind the pop-culture versions of samurai and ninja.

¥3,300 adults | ¥2,700 kids

📍 View Samurai Ninja Museum on Google Maps

Ganso Sample Making (Optional)

A playful workshop where families make ultra-realistic fake food replicas — the same wax models you see in restaurant window displays across Japan. Sessions run about 40 minutes and make for a genuinely fun keepsake. Located in Kappabashi, "Kitchen Town," a 12–15 min walk from Asakusa Station. Optional — but the group will stop in at the store regardless of who's booked a session, since the displays themselves are worth seeing.

~¥3,300 per person (standard items: tempura or lettuce) | ~40 minutes

⚠️ No walk-ins — a reservation is required for everyone, no exceptions. Each family should book their own spot directly: book online for groups of 1–4, or call 0120-17-1839 for groups of 5 or more.

Senso-ji Temple — Night Visit (~7:30 PM)

Tokyo's oldest temple, and genuinely a different place after dark — the Kaminarimon Gate's giant lantern, the five-story pagoda, and the main hall are all illuminated, the crowds thin out, and it makes for some of the best photos of the week. A relaxed, no-agenda stop to close out the day.

Free | Temple grounds open 24 hours | Nightly illumination runs until 11:00 PM

⚠️ Nakamise shopping street (our lunch spot) closes up for the night well before this — most stalls shut by early evening, so this visit is for the temple grounds themselves, not shopping or food.

📍 View Senso-ji Temple on Google Maps

Heritage & Craft Demos

Nihon Minkaen (Open-Air Folk House Museum)

An open-air museum of 25+ preserved Edo-period farmhouses relocated from around Japan, set in a wooded park. Sundays are extra special here — volunteer craftspeople demonstrate traditional skills like straw and bamboo work, weaving, and hearth-fire cooking, so kids get to see (and sometimes try) real historical trades in action.

¥550 adults | Free for youth

Address: 7-1-1 Masugata, Tama-ku, Kawasaki

📍 View Nihon Minkaen on Google Maps

Activity Details

Hamarikyu Gardens

A 350-year-old feudal lord's garden built around a genuine tidal pond — the water level actually rises and falls with Tokyo Bay. A teahouse sits on a small island in the pond, where you can sit for matcha and a seasonal sweet, plus wide lawns for kids to explore.

General: ¥300 | Ages 65+: ¥150 | Free for elementary-age and younger

Minato Science Museum

A small, interactive science center in central Tokyo — hands-on exhibits that work well for curious kids without needing a full-day commitment. Permanent exhibits are free, so this is an easy, low-cost stop between the gardens and the tower.

Permanent exhibits: Free

Address: 3-6-9 Toranomon  |  Nearest Station: Toranomon Hills Station (Tokyo Metro Hibiya Line), ~4 min walk

📍 View Minato Science Museum on Google Maps

Tokyo Tower (Optional Add-On)

Optional — this is the end of a closing-week day, and plenty of families will be ready to call it by this point. No pressure to make this one; the loop works fine ending at the Science Museum too.

Tokyo's original observation tower — a great way to close out the week with an orientation view of the whole city.

Main Deck (150m): Adults ¥1,500 | High school ¥1,200 | Elementary/Jr. High ¥900 | Ages 4+ (preschool) ¥600 | Under 4: Free
